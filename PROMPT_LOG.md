# About-section prompt log

This log records the AI-assisted drafting process used for the About section. The final copy was edited before it was placed in [aboutme.html](./aboutme.html).

## 1. Exact prompt

> Context: I am Elson Chakonza, a Year 2 Software Engineering and Computer Science student completing a Web Technologies profile page. Goal: draft a short first-person About section that explains what I study and what I am practising. Constraints: use plain language, sound like a real student rather than a company, avoid exaggerated claims and generic filler, mention semantic HTML, accessible interaction, and maintainable code, and keep the response to two short paragraphs. Format: return only the draft copy.

## 2. Raw AI output

I am a passionate Software Engineering and Computer Science student who loves creating innovative digital solutions and exploring the latest technologies. I enjoy building meaningful websites that are user-friendly, responsive, and visually appealing, and I am always eager to expand my knowledge and take on new challenges.

I am currently developing my skills in semantic HTML, CSS, and JavaScript while learning how to create accessible and maintainable interfaces. My goal is to become a versatile software engineer who combines creativity, technical knowledge, and a commitment to excellence in every project.

## 3. Final edited version

I am studying software engineering and computer science, with a growing interest in how thoughtful HTML, CSS, and JavaScript can make useful ideas easier to access.

I am working on clearer structure, accessible interaction, and maintainable code. Each small project gives me a chance to practise explaining decisions instead of only making a page look finished.

## 4. Reflection

I removed broad claims such as “passionate,” “innovative,” and “commitment to excellence” because they did not show what I am actually learning. I made the copy more specific to this course by naming semantic HTML, accessible interaction, and maintainable code, then shortened it into two paragraphs that fit the page and sound more like a student describing current practice.
